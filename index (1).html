About the Film

A story that closes in the way a room does.

Marko is a wealthy and respected marketing strategist who has built his identity on control, dominance and success. Behind the polished exterior, he is emotionally detached and devoid of empathy. When his wife and young son begin to feel like obstacles to his ambitions, he eliminates them without remorse, just as he did everyone else, convinced that he has solved the problem permanently.

After their death, Marko fights the law and justice with all the money and power he has at his disposal and evades the punishment. Just as he thinks that he is forever done with them, they continue to live in his new house, as before. As the narrative progresses, Marko’s behavior becomes increasingly bizarre. He begins writing on the walls, covering them with papers, removing elements of the house, and ultimately abandoning the basic conditions for living, sleeping inside the refrigerator. These actions are not merely the result of psychological disorder, but a desperate attempt to escape from Ana, from Luka, and from all the victims.

After 41 days spent in agony, Marko tries to take his own life.

30 hours later, he wakes up in a hospital, handcuffed to the bed. A team of top surgeons fought to save his life so that Marko can learn the truth; he has been sentenced to life imprisonment for twenty two murders, for which complete evidence now exists. The irony lies in the bullet, the bullet lodged deep into his brain causing amnesia, so he remembers neither the crimes, nor the house, nor his wife, nor his child, not even the woman who sold him the house, who we discover only at the very end that is the same person who, six years earlier, led the murder investigation as an inspector. The escape and the money that once allowed him to evade justice now end up in the wrong hands and are the reason his punishment is long, precise, and merciless.
